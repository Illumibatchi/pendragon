**In-game year:** 488

## Summary
The company attends [[Uther Pendragon|Uther]]'s Easter feast at the [[White Tower]] in [[Londinium]] carrying more secrets than honors. [[Sir Madoc]]'s suspicious accord with [[Clovis]] and the [[Franks]], his unauthorized kingly posturing, and [[Millicent]]'s theft-and-swap of the runed sword leave treason sitting on both sides of the table. The feast grows worse when [[Duke Erof of Cornwall]] arrives with his wife [[Ygraine]], whose beauty immediately destabilizes the hall. Uther cannot take his eyes off her, and what begins as courtly tension slides into open predation.

The feast collapses into Pendragon-grade dignity: [[Sir Geraint]] breaks wind before [[Lady Rachel of Carbonog]] and becomes the **Eastern Wind**; [[Millicent]] crashes into [[Lady Eadburh of the Vale]] and accidentally turns her apology into flirtation; [[Liam]] spends the feast trying to wingman everyone else while neglecting [[Damsel Diana]]; [[Assterius]] smooths that over; and [[Drustin]] defeats [[Sir Beefy]] so thoroughly in arm-wrestling that he breaks the man's arm. Beneath the comedy, dangerous truths surface. Drustin warns Millicent that Madoc may have fathered a bastard, though he first misidentifies the woman as [[Lady Dilwen]]. Millicent confronts Madoc, who admits the earlier incident involved [[Lady Rhianneth]] and says he does not know whether a child exists.

The political stakes sharpen. The company learns that [[King Brodin of Malahaut]], the Centurion-King, is fighting Saxons in the north with young [[King Lot of Lothian]], and that Uther has not answered their appeals. [[Duke Edaris of the March]] tells Liam that repeated Saxon raids may have ruined his lands beyond recovery; Liam secures [[Count Roderick of Salisbury|Roderick]]'s permission to assemble a Salisbury relief force. Meanwhile, Drustin tells [[Merlin]] the truth about the sword: the swap, the false blade, and the runed sword's return to the lake. Merlin is horrified and declares that Britain has **no sword** and **no king**.

After Drustin nearly repeats his [[Lady Ellen of Winchbank|Ellen]] disaster and is locked in an outhouse by Geraint and Assterius for everyone's good, the most important figures vanish from the feast. Merlin leaves first, then Ygraine, then an upset Uther, and finally Madoc with his squire. The company crosses into snowy Londinium and finds the trails split: Uther toward [[Llud’s Castle]], the Cornwalls toward the docks, and Madoc toward [[St. Mary Bethlehem]]. They follow Madoc and find him inside the nunnery with his head in a nun's lap. Millicent's religious knowledge confirms that whatever this is, it is **not** a normal rite of the Church.

## Scene/Beat Log
- **Easter at the White Tower:** The company attends Uther's Easter feast at the [[White Tower]] in [[Londinium]].
- **Cornwall arrives:** [[Duke Erof of Cornwall]] appears outwardly reconciled with Uther and brings [[Ygraine]], whose presence immediately changes the room.
- **Ygraine's effect:** Millicent, Liam, Assterius, Geraint, and Drustin are all affected by Ygraine's beauty through adoration, jealousy, or emotional collapse.
- **Eastern Wind:** [[Sir Geraint]] breaks wind before [[Lady Rachel of Carbonog]] but later recovers socially and continues courting her.
- **Millicent and Eadburh:** [[Millicent]] trips into [[Lady Eadburh of the Vale]] during a dance; her apology is read as flirtation, and Eadburh is not offended.
- **Dance as pursuit:** Uther repeatedly uses dancing to move nearer to [[Ygraine]], eventually becoming visibly predatory.
- **Archbishop counsel:** [[Drustin]] seeks counsel from [[Archbishop Dubricus]] about faith, curses, and his possible werewolf taint.
- **Sir Beefy beaten:** Drustin defeats [[Sir Beefy]] in a test of strength and breaks his arm.
- **Madoc rumor:** Drustin warns Millicent that Madoc may have fathered a bastard, but initially names [[Lady Dilwen]] incorrectly.
- **Millicent confronts Madoc:** Millicent demands the truth; [[Sir Madoc]] admits [[Lady Rhianneth]] took advantage of him while he was drunk, but says he does not know whether a child exists.
- **Dilwen corrected:** Drustin later concludes Dilwen does not appear to have recently borne a child, and the Rhianneth correction stands.
- **Northern crisis:** The company hears that [[King Brodin of Malahaut]] is fighting Saxons with [[King Lot of Lothian]], but Uther has not answered appeals for aid.
- **The March:** [[Duke Edaris of the March]] tells [[Liam]] his lands are devastated by Saxon raids and may not survive.
- **Relief force authorized:** Liam asks [[Count Roderick of Salisbury]] for permission to assemble a force for the March; Roderick agrees.
- **Merlin told:** Moved by love for [[Merlin]], Drustin tells him the truth about the sword swap and the runed blade's return to the lake.
- **No sword, no king:** Merlin is horrified and says Britain has no sword and no king before fleeing into the night.
- **Outhouse rescue:** Drustin drifts toward [[Lady Ellen of Winchbank]] again; Geraint and Assterius intercept him and lock him in an outhouse.
- **Departures:** Pages report that Merlin left first by boat, then Ygraine, then an upset Uther, then Madoc with his squire.
- **Snowy Londinium:** The company crosses into Londinium and tracks the departures through the snow.
- **Split trails:** Uther's tracks lead toward [[Llud’s Castle]], the Cornwalls' toward the docks, and Madoc's toward [[St. Mary Bethlehem]].
- **St. Mary Bethlehem:** The company follows Madoc and finds him with his head in a nun's lap; Millicent recognizes this is not a normal Church rite.

## Consequences & New Facts
- [[Ygraine]]'s arrival at court triggers the Uther/Cornwall crisis.
- [[Merlin]] now knows the real sword was returned to the lake and that Madoc bears a false blade.
- [[Lady Rhianneth]], not [[Lady Dilwen]], is the corrected woman in Madoc's prior scandal; whether a child exists is unknown.
- [[Duke Edaris of the March]]'s lands are near collapse from Saxon raids.
- [[King Brodin of Malahaut]] and [[King Lot of Lothian]] are fighting Saxons without Uther's help.
- The session ends at [[St. Mary Bethlehem]], with Madoc apparently compromised again.
