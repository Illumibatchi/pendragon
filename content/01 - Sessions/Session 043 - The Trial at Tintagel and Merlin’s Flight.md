**In-game year:** 492

## Summary
The treason case against the player knights reaches its climax at Tintagel. Sir Geraint defends his honor, while Liam and Drustin fail attempts to make their defenses fully convincing and Assterius fails to deliver his eloquent speech mechanically. Millicent successfully agitates the gallery in the knights’ favor. Uther dismisses the charges and the knights are acquitted, but Sir Brastias remains convinced that Merlin and Drustin are behind the plot. Ygraine privately asks Millicent to do for her son what Millicent did for her daughter, leaving Millicent determined to protect the child.

The party investigates the handmaid Emma, who had provided information about Arthur, and finds that she has died after being run down by a horse in the high street. Her friend Gabriella reports that Bruce and his men have repeatedly questioned the servants. The party arranges for Gabriella to leave secretly and travel toward Exmoor. At the Exmoor standing stones, the siren reports that four heavily equipped men with twelve horses searched the stones and the nearby siren stones before heading toward Tintagel. Merlin then appears in the forest with Arthur and escapes during a confrontation with Bruce and Brastias. Drustin tracks Merlin to a pool and hears Merlin argue that the good of the realm is the continuation of the Pendragon bloodline. Drustin returns without Arthur, carrying only a goose, and accepts Merlin’s argument that the natural order should proceed. The party reunites near Glastonbury divided over whether Merlin is wise or dangerous; Arthur remains with Merlin and his whereabouts are unknown.

## Scene/Beat Log
- **Trial at Tintagel:** Brastias presents the accusations of treason, aiding Merlin, and involvement in the missing heir’s disappearance before Uther.
- **Defenses:** Geraint succeeds on Honor; Liam fails Deceitful; Drustin fails Deceitful; Assterius fails Orate. Millicent succeeds at Intrigue and agitates the gallery in the party’s defense.
- **Acquittal:** Uther dismisses the charges and allows the knights to leave. Drustin’s request for a private audience with Uther is denied.
- **Ygraine’s plea:** Ygraine privately asks Millicent to do for her son what she did for her daughter. She appears desperate and emotionally broken.
- **Brastias’s position:** Brastias says he believes Merlin and Drustin are behind the plot and treats Drustin as Merlin’s chief minion.
- **Emma:** Geraint locates Emma, the local handmaid who had supplied information about Arthur. Emma is dead; her parents say she was run over by a horse in the high street, with no known rider or witness. A white mourning ribbon hangs on the family door.
- **Kitchen witnesses:** Emma’s friends are Fiona, a former maid now married to a knight in Orkney, and Gabriella, the kitchen matron. Gabriella says Bruce and his men have questioned nearly all the servants repeatedly. She agrees to leave under cover of darkness, and the party brings her toward Exmoor.
- **Clerical counsel:** Dubricus is unavailable, and the Bishop of Exeter is reported dead from the war. Stronger counsel may instead be found at Taunton or with Abbot-Bishop Gwion at Glastonbury.
- **Exmoor search:** A mounted party has searched the Standing Stones of Exmoor and the siren stones. The tracks suggest four men with twelve horses, heavy equipment, and movement from the north/east toward Tintagel.
- **Siren information:** The siren does not know where Merlin is, but mentions a possible abode near the great snowy mountains, perhaps Mount Snowdon. This remains an unconfirmed lead.
- **Merlin’s appearance:** Merlin emerges from the forest with Arthur and calls on Drustin to delay the pursuers, warning that the fate of the kingdom is at stake.
- **Roadside confrontation:** Bruce, Brastias, and three other knights confront the party. Drustin draws his sword and defends against several bodyguards. Bruce draws a cat-o’-nine-tails. Geraint succeeds at Orate and convinces Brastias to cease hostilities before the encounter becomes a full battle.
- **Merlin’s escape:** Merlin uses Glamour and the confusion of the confrontation to escape. Millicent and Assterius lose the trail; Drustin successfully tracks Merlin to a pool.
- **The pool:** Merlin temporarily hands Arthur to Drustin while demonstrating how to milk a goose. Merlin says that the good of the realm is the continuation of the Pendragon bloodline and that the land is the king and the king is the land. Drustin returns with the goose rather than Arthur and accepts Merlin’s reasoning.
- **Party division:** Millicent remains committed to finding and protecting Arthur. Liam distrusts Merlin. Geraint believes Merlin may be wiser than the knights. Drustin presents Merlin’s explanation favorably and argues that natural order, rather than immediate recovery of the child, is the goal.
