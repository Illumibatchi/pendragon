## Notes
Player-facing reference from Cai’s table clarification on what exists and what does **not** exist in the King Uther Period. If something is not listed here, assume it is not standard for this period unless the GM says otherwise.

## Customs
- **Might makes right.** The world is practical, brutal, and violent.
- **The Truce of God** exists as a Church effort to limit violence against innocents, protect holy days, and protect Church property, but most nobles and knights ignore it.
- **Knighthood** emphasizes loyalty to one’s lord, willingness to face enemies, and readiness to deal out death.
- **Chivalry** is known from bardic stories, especially stories of Alexander the Great, but it is not a lived social code in the later Arthurian sense.
- Knights generally treat fellow warriors with respect and civility, but almost no one thinks that courtesy should extend to Saxons or enemies.
- **Romance is unknown** as a cultural mode; courtship and marriage are better understood through status, family, politics, permission, and alliance.

## Equipment
- **Armor:** Norman **10-point armor** is the standard available armor.
- **Helmets:** Open helmets with nose guards.
- **Shields:** Long shields are the rule.
- **Unavailable weapons:** flail, great sword, halberd, hammer, jousting lance, morning star, war flail.
- **Horses:** Chargers are the best available horses but are specialized and exceptional, not available to most martial men. Rouncys are the common warrior’s steed.
- **Horse colors:** For visual description, most horses are shades of brown, with recognized names such as dun and chestnut.
- **Clothing:** Fifth-century native dress is standard.

## Castles and fortifications
- **Motte-and-bailey castles** are prevalent.
- Some cities have **stone walls**, mostly inherited from Roman construction.
- Stone castles are very rare and legendary in their uniqueness, such as the [[White Tower]].

## Play implications
- Avoid assuming later-medieval courtly romance, tournament culture, or chivalric mercy as defaults.
- Treat “honorable knight” behavior as loyalty, courage, and controlled violence first; mercy and courtesy to enemies are exceptional rather than assumed.
- Check weapon descriptions against the unavailable list before recording a PC or NPC using a later-period weapon.
- Treat chargers as notable possessions, not default warhorses.
